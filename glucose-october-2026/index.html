About this data

Context. Recorded after a lab A1c of 5.7% and a lab glucose of 111 mg/dL. Lower-carbohydrate eating with two regular foods: dinner (0% Greek yogurt with extra-virgin olive oil, usually 7–8:30 pm) and Cocoa + Protein (Guittard Cocoa Rouge with NOW Amino 9 essential amino acid powder) taken at various times, including overnight. Exercises daily (running, cycling, water running or swimming, not all logged) and works at a treadmill desk, so light walking is frequent and unlogged.

Patient-reported symptoms. Often wakes overnight in a cold sweat after a very low reading, and usually takes Cocoa + Protein then. Feels tired or cold when glucose is falling. Glucose drifts down when sitting on the bed, resting or going to sleep, and sometimes dips briefly at the start of eating before rising. Treadmill-desk walking raises it slightly, typically into the 90s.

GMI (glucose management indicator) estimates A1c from CGM mean glucose: 3.31 + 0.02392 × mean. It is designed for at least 14 days of data, so a shorter window is only indicative.

Below-range readings. The sensor reports “Out of range” when glucose is under about 55 mg/dL. These are drawn as red ticks along the bottom of the chart, counted in time below 70, and left out of averages, so the mean is slightly overstated. The patient sleeps on their back, so sensor compression (a false low from lying on the sensor) is unlikely, and several overnight lows came with waking in a cold sweat. A fingerstick during a low alert would confirm the sensor readings.

Typical day is an ambulatory glucose profile: every day stacked on one 24-hour axis, showing the median and the middle 50% and 90% of readings at each time.

Readings every 5 minutes from an over-the-counter Lingo sensor. Period boundaries: overnight 10 pm–6 am, morning 6–11 am, daytime 11 am–7 pm, dinner 7–10 pm.
